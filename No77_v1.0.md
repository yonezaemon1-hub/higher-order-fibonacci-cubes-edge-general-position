## Model and theorem

For integers k>=3 and n>=2 let G=Q_n(1^k) be the subgraph of the binary n-cube induced by the words without k consecutive ones. Write A_n=A_n^(k) for their number, A_0=1. An edge general position set S has no three distinct edges on a common shortest path. Denote its maximum cardinality by gp_e(G). Let Theta_i be the edges flipping coordinate i.

**Theorem.** For all k>=3 and n>=2, gp_e(G)=2(A_n-A_(n-1)). If n>=k+1 the unique maximum set is Theta_1 union Theta_n. If 2<=n<=k, precisely the unions of two distinct coordinate classes are maximum sets.

No statement for k=2 is assumed: the proof is independent of No.75 and No.76.

## 1. Isometry and coordinate counts

The legal-word family is downward closed in the coordinatewise order. Between any legal x,y, go from x down to x AND y, then up to y, flipping each differing bit once. Every intermediate word is legal, hence graph distance equals Hamming distance. In particular a geodesic never repeats a coordinate; a union of two coordinate classes is in edge general position.

Every legal word with bit i=1 corresponds bijectively to its downward edge in Theta_i. The words with bit i=0 split independently into two legal words, so

|Theta_i| = A_n - A_(i-1) A_(n-i).

Concatenation embeds the legal words of length n-1 into the pairs of legal words of lengths i-1,n-i. Consequently A_(i-1) A_(n-i)>=A_(n-1). For i=1,n equality holds, and B=2(A_n-A_(n-1)) is a lower bound for gp_e(G).

If n>=k+1 and 1<i<n, choose positive integers a<=i-1,b<=n-i with a,b<=k-1 and a+b=k. Such integers exist since both lengths are positive and their sum is n-1>=k. A legal left word ending in a ones and a legal right word starting in b ones (all other bits zero) have illegal concatenation. Thus the inequality is strict for every interior i. When n<=k, A_(n-1)=2^(n-1) and both factors are powers of two, so all coordinate classes have equal size.

## 2. Two-arm projection lemma

In any isometric induced subgraph of a hypercube, suppose a selected star at c contains edges in distinct coordinates i,j. A selected edge e in a coordinate l different from i,j cannot have projection on i,j equal to either of the two leaves c^i or c^j (superscript means flip that coordinate).

Proof: choose that leaf z, and the endpoint w of e with w_l=z_l. By hypothesis z and w agree on i,j,l, so any shortest path joining z and w flips none of these coordinates. Concatenate the other leaf -> c -> z, a shortest z-w path, and e. All coordinate flips occur at most once, and because graph distances are Hamming distances this is a geodesic containing three selected edges. Contradiction.

## 3. Star forest lemma

If S uses at least three distinct coordinates, the graph whose edges are S is a star forest (ignore isolated vertices).

Proof: if a connected component contains a simple three-edge path, its directions must repeat (otherwise the path is a geodesic). As consecutive directions cannot repeat in a simple path, the pattern is i,j,i. The two consecutive two-arm stars have respective leaf projections covering all four binary patterns on i,j (the four vertices form three sides of an ambient square). A selected edge in a third coordinate is therefore forbidden by Section 2. This contradicts the hypothesis. Hence there is no simple path of length three. The graph is bipartite. Any connected bipartite graph with no simple three-edge path is a star: a cycle would contain such a path, and a tree of diameter at most two is a star.

In particular, with largest star degree d, |S|<=d/(d+1) A_n.

## 4. Uniform density of the two end classes

For n<k, A_n=2^n and B=A_n. For n=k, A_n=2^k-1 and B=2^k-2. For n>=k+1, counting legal words of length n-1 extended by one bit gives A_n=2 A_(n-1)-A_(n-k-1): the forbidden extensions of otherwise legal words are exactly the words ending in 0 followed by k ones, with an arbitrary legal prefix of length n-k-1. Hence B=A_n-A_(n-k-1).

Claim: B>=10 A_n/11 for all n>=4, k>=3, with strict inequality for n>=10.

For k>=4 and n>=k+1, from each legal prefix of length n-k-1 append a zero and any legal word of length k. This gives A_n >= (2^k-1) A_(n-k-1) >=15 A_(n-k-1). The cases n<=k are immediate from the explicit counts (n=k>=4). For k=3, A_n satisfies A_n=A_(n-1)+A_(n-2)+A_(n-3) for n>=3. The inequality A_n>=11 A_(n-4) at n=4,5,6 follows from A_4=13,A_5=24,A_6=44 and A_0=1,A_1=2,A_2=4. The difference satisfies the same recurrence from n=7 onward, hence is strictly positive for every n>=7. This proves the claim.

## 5. Large stars exclude a fixed fraction of vertices

Suppose d>=11. Among the d coordinates of a largest star at c, choose six pairwise nonconsecutive coordinates: sorting the d coordinates and selecting alternating ones gives at least ceil(d/2)>=6. Split the chosen coordinates into two triples.

Let X be a uniformly random legal vertex. Conditional on any specified values of some other chosen coordinates (with nonempty conditioning event), for any still-unfixed chosen coordinate i we have

1/5 <= Pr(X_i=1 | condition) <= 1/2.

Indeed, deleting a one at i is an injection from the conditioned words with X_i=1 into those with X_i=0. Conversely, set bit i to one and clear both immediate neighbors (only existing neighbors at boundaries). This creates an isolated one, preserves legality for k>=2, and preserves the conditioning since no chosen coordinate is adjacent to i. This map from the zero class into the one class has at most four preimages per image (the former neighboring bits). Thus #ones <= #zeros <=4 #ones. Both classes are nonempty. It follows that the disagreement indicator Y_i=1_{X_i != c_i} has conditional probabilities for either value at least 1/5 and at most 4/5.

In either triple, conditional on the first disagreement indicator and any earlier specified chosen bits, the probability that both remaining indicators equal the first is at most (4/5)^2. Thus the probability that the triple is mixed (contains both zero and one) is at least 9/25. Conditioning on every possible full assignment of the first triple, the same bound holds for the second. Therefore the probability that both triples are mixed is at least (9/25)^2=81/625.

For any vertex x in this event, its projection on the d star coordinates has at least two agreements and at least two disagreements with c. For any incident selected edge in direction l, remove l if it is a star coordinate. At least one agreeing and one disagreeing star coordinate remain; call them i,j. The edge has the projected pattern of one of the two leaves c^i,c^j on this pair. Section 2 forbids that edge. Hence none of these vertices is incident to S.

At least 81/625 of the vertices are excluded. As S is a star forest, its number of edges is at most its number of incident vertices. In particular |S| <= 544 A_n/625 < 10 A_n/11 <= B (n>=11).

## 6. Completion and equality

If S uses at most two coordinates, Section 1 bounds its size by B; equality requires two complete largest coordinate classes. If S uses at least three, it is a star forest. When d>=11 Section 5 gives a strict bound below B. When d<=10, |S|<=d A_n/(d+1)<=10 A_n/11. For n>=4 the bound is strict below B: if n<=9 then d<=n<=9 and the degree ratio is strictly below 10/11; if n>=10 Section 4 gives B>10 A_n/11. For n=3, d<=3 and B is 6 for k=3, 8 for k>3; 3 A_n/4 < B in both cases. For n=2 at least three coordinates are impossible. These cases exhaust all sets. The equality classification follows from Section 1. QED.
